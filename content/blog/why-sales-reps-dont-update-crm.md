## CRM adoption: why sales reps don't update the CRM, and how to fix it

Sales reps don't update the CRM because typing up a call costs them time today and pays them back, if at all, in someone else's review meeting. **CRM adoption** improves when the record fills itself from work reps already do: emails, calls and notes land on the customer timeline on their own, and the rep's job shrinks to checking and confirming. Training and reminders help at the edges. Removing the typing fixes the cause.

## What is CRM adoption?

CRM adoption is how consistently your team keeps the CRM current. Every lead, call, email, quote and next step that matters should be on the record soon after it happens. Logins are a weak measure. A rep can open the CRM every day and still keep the real pipeline in a notebook or a spreadsheet.

A better test is simple. Pick five live deals at random. Can someone other than the owner tell, from the record alone, what the buyer last said and what happens next? If not, adoption is low, whatever the usage report says.

## Why don't sales reps update the CRM?

Mostly because data entry is work on top of the work. Salesforce's seventh [State of Sales report](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/reports/sales/salesforce-state-of-sales-report-2026.pdf) surveyed 4,050 sales professionals in 22 countries, India among them, in August and September 2025. Reps said they spend 40% of an average workweek selling. Manually entering data took 13%, alongside creating quotes at 17%, planning at 16% and training at 11%.

![Bar chart of how sales reps spend an average workweek, from Salesforce's State of Sales, seventh edition: selling 40%, made up of meeting customers 22% and prospecting 18%; creating quotes 17%; planning 16%; manually entering data 13%; training 11%; other 3%](/images/blog/why-sales-reps-dont-update-crm-workweek.webp)

Time is only part of it. These are the patterns that keep records empty:

- **The effort comes first, the benefit comes later.** The rep types the notes now. The manager reads them on Friday.
- **Updates happen after the moment.** A call ends, the next one starts, and notes get written from memory at the end of the day, or not at all.
- **Forms ask for what nobody knows yet.** A close date and a probability on a deal that started yesterday invite guesses, and guesses make the data look worse than blank.
- **Conversations live somewhere else.** Email, the phone and chat each hold part of the deal. The CRM gets a summary if anyone has time.
- **The CRM only shows up in bad news.** If the record is opened only to ask why a number was missed, reps learn to update it the night before the review, and optimistically.

That last pattern shows up in the research. In Validity's State of CRM Data Management in 2025, a survey of 602 CRM users and administrators in the US, UK and Australia, 37% said staff regularly fabricate data to tell leaders what they want to hear ([Validity, via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/validity-releases-state-of-crm-data-management-in-2025-report-revealing-disconnect-between-data-quality-and-ai-implementation-302499899.html)).

Managers feel it too. In a [HubSpot Community thread](https://community.hubspot.com/t/how-do-you-get-sales-teams-to-log-activities-in-hubspot/160348), a manager asks how other teams got account teams past their reluctance to log every call and email. The answers there point the same way as this post: connect email so it logs itself, and only ask people to record what matters, such as decisions and next steps.

## What does poor CRM adoption cost?

It costs you a pipeline you cannot trust. Forecasts drift, follow-ups slip because nobody wrote down the promise, and handovers turn into phone calls because the record says nothing useful.

The [same Validity survey](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/validity-releases-state-of-crm-data-management-in-2025-report-revealing-disconnect-between-data-quality-and-ai-implementation-302499899.html) found that 76% of respondents said less than half of their organisation's CRM data is accurate and complete. Workers spent an average of 13 hours a week hunting for basic information in the CRM. [Salesforce's report](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/reports/sales/salesforce-state-of-sales-report-2026.pdf) lists the top data issues among sales teams using AI agents as manual errors, duplicate data and incomplete data.

That last finding matters more each year. AI agents read the same records your team does. If the record is thin, an agent drafting a reply or a deal summary works from the same gaps, which is one reason [AI sales agents make things up](https://heigin.com/blog/ai-sales-agent-hallucinations).

## How do you get sales reps to use the CRM?

Make the record cheaper to keep than to skip. Mandates and dashboards push in the right direction for a few weeks. Lasting adoption comes from taking the typing away and giving reps something back.

1. **Capture activity automatically.** Connect email so customer threads land on the timeline without forwarding or copy and paste. Do the same for calls where your phone setup allows it.
2. **Ask for confirmation, not composition.** Let the system draft the call summary, the next step and the task. Checking three lines takes seconds. Writing them from memory takes minutes and rarely happens.
3. **Cut required fields to the ones that drive a decision.** Owner, stage, value, next step and its date. Leave the rest optional until someone actually knows it.
4. **Tie stage changes to buyer actions.** A deal moves when the buyer does something, not when the rep feels good about it. We cover exit rules in [sales pipeline stages](https://heigin.com/blog/sales-pipeline-stages).
5. **Run every review from the CRM screen.** If a deal is not on the record, it is not discussed. People update what gets looked at.
6. **Give something back the same day.** A list of who to call today, or a short brief before a meeting. When the CRM helps a rep sell, they keep it current for their own sake.
7. **Measure freshness, not logins.** Count deals with no next step, and deals untouched for longer than their stage allows.

## Can AI fix CRM data entry?

AI can take on most of the typing, but not the judgement. It can transcribe and summarise calls, pull the next step out of an email thread, and notice when a deal has gone quiet. What it should not do is write freely to your records with no one checking.

Three rules keep AI-written data trustworthy:

- **Show the source.** Every summary or field change should say which call, email or note it came from, so anyone can check it.
- **Keep a person on changes that matter.** Stage moves, values and anything customer-facing should wait for a confirm.
- **Log who approved what.** If a record changed, you should be able to see whether a person or an agent did it, and who signed off.

Get those right and AI becomes the reason reps stop skipping updates, not another thing they have to clean up after. We go deeper on this in [how we think about agent trust](https://heigin.com/blog/how-we-think-about-agent-trust).

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do salespeople hate updating the CRM?

Because it usually means retyping what they already said on a call or in an email, at a time when they would rather be on the next call. The benefit goes mostly to managers and forecasts, not to the rep. Capturing activity automatically and giving reps something useful back, such as a daily call list, changes that balance.

### How do you measure CRM adoption?

Measure how current the records are, not how often people log in. Useful checks are the share of open deals with a dated next step, the number of deals untouched for longer than their stage allows, and whether a random deal's record tells a colleague what the buyer last said. Track these weekly and review them as a team.

### How often should sales reps update the CRM?

The same day, and ideally as the activity happens. Notes written at the end of the week are written from memory and miss the details that matter, such as dates and promised documents. When email and calls are captured automatically, the rep only needs to confirm what was drafted.

### Can AI update the CRM automatically?

Yes, AI can transcribe calls, summarise emails and suggest next steps and tasks. The safe setup shows the source of every entry, keeps a person on stage changes and customer-facing actions, and logs who approved each change. That keeps the speed of automation without filling the record with confident mistakes.

### Should CRM updates be mandatory for sales reps?

A short list of required fields helps: owner, stage, value and the next step with a date. Making everything mandatory backfires, because reps fill fields with guesses to get past the form. Keep the required list small and make the rest of the record fill itself from email and calls.
