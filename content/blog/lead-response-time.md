## Lead response time: how fast should you reply to a new lead?

Reply within minutes when a lead is comparing suppliers, and within the same working day when it is not. **Lead response time** is the gap between a buyer sending an enquiry and your first real reply, and the best-known research found that the odds of reaching a web lead fall sharply within the first half hour. Most companies are far slower than that. You do not need more people to fix it: you need every lead in one place, a clear owner, ready answers and a person checking each reply.

## What is lead response time?

Lead response time is the time from when a lead arrives to the first real attempt to talk to them. A real attempt is a call, or a reply written for that buyer. An automatic "thanks, we'll be in touch" email does not count, because it answers nothing.

Two details make the number honest:

- **Start the clock when the enquiry arrives**, not when someone opens it. The form submit time, the missed-call time or the email's received time is the start.
- **Use the median, not the average.** A few leads that waited a week drag the average up. XANT's lead response report uses the median for that reason ([XANT, 2014 Lead Response Report](https://resources.insidesales.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/2014-Lead-Response-Report.pdf)).

Track it by lead source too, because a demo request and a referral behave differently.

## What does the research say about speed to lead?

Fast replies reach far more leads, and most companies reply slowly. The studies are older and mostly American, so treat the figures as direction, not law.

- **The MIT study.** In 2007, Dr James Oldroyd and InsideSales.com looked at three years of data from six companies: over 15,000 web leads and over 100,000 call attempts. The odds of contacting a lead called within 5 minutes were 100 times higher than at 30 minutes, and the odds of qualifying it were 21 times higher. Even going from 5 to 10 minutes cut the odds of contact by a factor of five ([InsideSales.com/MIT Lead Response Management Study](https://www.mortech.com/hs-fs/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf)).
- **The Harvard Business Review audit.** "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads" appeared in [HBR in March 2011](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads). As summarised in XANT's 2014 report, only 37% of companies replied within an hour, 24% took more than a day, and the average first response was 42 hours ([XANT](https://resources.insidesales.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/2014-Lead-Response-Report.pdf); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/geoffrey-james/inbound-marketing-how-to-make-it-more-effective.html)).
- **XANT's 2014 report.** A test lead went to 9,538 companies through their web forms. 47% never responded. Among those that called, the median first call came after 3 hours and 8 minutes ([XANT](https://resources.insidesales.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/2014-Lead-Response-Report.pdf)).
- **Workato's test.** Workato filled in demo requests at 114 B2B companies. Only one sent a personalised email within 5 minutes, and the average personalised email took 11 hours and 54 minutes. Just 31% called at all, and none called within 5 minutes ([Workato](https://www.workato.com/the-connector/lead-response-time-study/)).

The MIT study also found something people quote less often. After about 20 hours, each extra call made it harder, not easier, to qualify the lead ([MIT study](https://www.mortech.com/hs-fs/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf)). Early effort counts for more than late persistence.

## Is the five-minute rule still true?

The five-minute rule holds best for leads who are contacting several suppliers at once. It holds least for referrals and repeat customers, who were going to talk to you anyway.

Practitioners land in the same place. In a [r/smallbusiness thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/smallbusiness/comments/1v2cytv/whats_actually_a_good_response_time_to_an_inbound/) asking whether anyone had tested the rule, replies said speed matters most when a buyer has sent the same enquiry to several businesses, and far less for warm leads. One reply said the bigger gain is going from "end of day" to within half an hour, not from five minutes to one.

The thread made one more useful point: a lead that went cold because nobody replied often gets marked "bad lead" in the CRM, so the report never shows the delay.

## How fast should you respond to each type of lead?

Set a target for each kind of lead, based on how likely the buyer is to be talking to someone else right now. These are working targets, not research findings, so adjust them with your own data.

![Decision guide for lead response targets. If the buyer is comparing suppliers, as with ad lead forms, enquiries sent to several sellers and missed calls, reply within minutes during working hours. If the buyer asked you directly, as with quote requests, demo requests and website forms, reply within the hour. If the buyer already knows you, as with referrals and repeat customers, reply the same working day. Enquiries after hours get a real reply first thing the next working morning](/images/blog/lead-response-time-targets.webp)

- **Comparing suppliers: within minutes.** Ad lead forms, enquiries posted to several sellers and missed calls. The first useful reply often gets the conversation.
- **Asked you directly: within the hour.** Quote requests, demo requests and website forms. The buyer chose you, but they still have options.
- **Already knows you: same working day.** Referrals and repeat customers.
- **After hours: first thing next morning.** A real reply, not a template.

## Why are sales teams slow to respond to leads?

Teams are slow because leads arrive in too many places and nobody owns the first reply:

- **Leads land in different inboxes.** Ad forms, email, phone and marketplaces each sit in a different tab or app.
- **No owner.** A lead that belongs to "the team" belongs to nobody in the first ten minutes.
- **The answer needs information.** Price, stock, delivery or a brochure, and the rep has to find it first.
- **No one can tell who is hot.** A serious buyer waits in the same queue as a student asking for a project report.

## How do you improve lead response time?

Remove the steps between the enquiry arriving and the reply going out. None of these needs extra headcount.

1. **Bring every lead source into one inbox.** If ad forms, email and missed calls land in the same place, nothing waits in a tab nobody checks.
2. **Give every lead an owner the moment it arrives.** Decide the rule in advance, by territory, product or rota, so nobody has to decide on the spot.
3. **Answer the hottest leads first.** A reply that names a quantity and a deadline should not wait behind a vague question. We cover how to read this in [AI lead scoring](https://heigin.com/blog/ai-lead-scoring-buying-intent).
4. **Prepare approved answers.** Keep your current offer, prices, FAQs and brochures ready, so the first reply can actually answer the question.
5. **Let AI draft, and a person send.** A draft built from your approved material, checked by the owner, goes out in a minute or two.
6. **Plan the second and third touch.** One call and one email is where most teams stop. Our post on [why follow-ups slip](https://heigin.com/blog/why-follow-ups-slip) covers keeping those promises visible.
7. **Review the median every week, by source.** Look at the slowest source first. That is usually where the leads are being lost.

Consider a fictional company, Northwind Traders. A buyer at Sundaram Mills fills in an ad lead form at 7:40 pm asking for a price on 2,000 metres of cotton, delivered by month end. In the old setup, the form sits in an email folder until the rep checks it after lunch the next day, by which point the buyer has two other quotes. In the new setup, the lead lands in the team inbox with the form answers attached and an owner already named. Her first job next morning is a reply drafted from the current price list, which she checks and sends before 10 am.

## Can AI respond to leads automatically?

AI can draft the first reply in seconds, but a person should check anything that quotes a price, promises a date or commits the business. A fast wrong answer costs more than a slightly slower right one.

The safe setup: the AI writes only from material your business has approved, checks its claims against those sources, and the owner reviews and sends. We go into the failure modes in [how to stop AI sales agents making things up](https://heigin.com/blog/ai-sales-agent-hallucinations).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good lead response time?

For leads who are likely contacting several suppliers, such as ad form leads and missed calls, aim for a real reply within minutes during working hours. For direct quote or demo requests, within the hour is a sound target. Referrals and repeat customers can usually wait until later the same working day.

### What is the five-minute rule in sales?

It is the idea that you should contact a new lead within five minutes. It comes mainly from a 2007 InsideSales.com and MIT study, which found the odds of contacting a web lead were 100 times higher at 5 minutes than at 30. It matters most for leads who are comparing several suppliers at the same time.

### How do you calculate lead response time?

Take the time each enquiry arrived and the time of the first real call or written reply, and find the difference. Report the median across all leads, split by lead source, every week. Do not count automatic acknowledgement emails as a response.

### Does an autoresponder count as a lead response?

No. An automatic acknowledgement tells the buyer you received the enquiry, but it does not answer their question or start a conversation. Measure response time to the first call or personalised reply, and use the autoresponder only to set expectations about when that will come.

### Should AI reply to new leads automatically?

AI is useful for drafting a fast first reply, as long as it writes only from your approved offer, FAQs and documents. A person should still review anything that includes a price, a delivery date or another commitment before it goes out. That keeps the speed without the risk of a confident wrong answer.

### How many times should you follow up a new lead?

More than once, and early. XANT's 2014 report found that the median company made just one contact attempt. The MIT study found that after about 20 hours, extra calls made qualifying the lead harder, so put your effort into the first day.
